## The map

Security law has four layers, and they apply at the same time:

| Layer | Examples | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criminal | StGB §§202a–c, CFAA, UK CMA | Is this access a crime? |
| Regulatory | NIS2, CRA, sector rules | What must organisations do? |
| Data protection | GDPR | How may personal data be processed? |
| Product/AI | CRA, AI Act | What must products and models satisfy? |

A penetration test can be lawful under criminal law and still breach GDPR if it processes
personal data carelessly.

## The international frame

The **Budapest Convention** (ETS No. 185, in force 2004) harmonises national computer-crime
offences and enables cross-border cooperation. Most European and many other states are parties.
It does not create a single offence; it obliges signatories to criminalise comparable conduct,
which is why the national statutes still matter.

## Three rules that cover most situations

1. **Permission in writing**, for named systems, for a defined window.
2. **Minimum data**: read what proves the finding, nothing more.
3. **Do not publish** before a fix or an agreed deadline.
